How KSEB solar net metering works

  • Bi-directional meter: records both energy imported from KSEB and excess solar exported back to the 230V/415V grid.
  • Net billing: if imports exceed exports, you pay energy charges only on the net imported units, at the LT-1A slabs.
  • Banked units: if exports exceed imports, the surplus is banked in your KSEB ledger and rolls over to offset later cycles.
  • Annual September settlement: at the end of the settlement year (September 30), any remaining banked units are settled by KSEB at the KERC-notified Average Power Purchase Cost (APPC).

The APPC settlement rate is revised periodically and reported differently across sources. When you check your numbers, use the rate from the latest KERC order rather than a blog figure.

Why your bill can still be non-zero with solar

Even with surplus solar, a KSEB bill keeps a bi-monthly fixed charge and meter rent, and any grid units imported during the night or on cloudy days are billed at slab rates. This tool separates self-consumed solar from grid import so you can see the true cost of running the home.

Frequently asked questions

What are banked units in KSEB solar net metering?

Surplus solar exported beyond your import in a cycle is stored as banked units and carried forward to offset future bills.

What happens at the September settlement?

Unadjusted banked units are settled at the KERC-notified APPC rate each September 30; the rate changes with each tariff order.
